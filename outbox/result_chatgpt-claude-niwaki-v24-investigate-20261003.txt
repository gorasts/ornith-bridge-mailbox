3. MTP / PLE: no MTP head in the Niwaki files. The Unsloth shared MTP module (mtp-...-shared-Q4_K_M, 1.9 GB) is a separate layer and could in principle draft for it (it reuses the main token_embd), but untested and Niwaki's narrowed experts may lower acceptance. PLE table is inside the GGUF -> must be mmap/lazy (like the ISTA coder); with dio it would be pulled into RAM.

4. RAM/VRAM on OLD GORAST (32 GB RAM, ~27 GB free): the smallest file (102B UD-Q3K, 43.9 GB) does NOT fit as resident experts. Even if the packed PLE table stays lazy, routed experts + backbone are well above ~27 GB -> continuous NVMe paging under mmap, i.e. TG collapse. VRAM side would be fine (backbone Q6_K on card 05 + MTP + KV within 8 GB with --cpu-moe-draft). VERDICT: not practical on the old board without more RAM; realistic on the MR9A with 96 GB (102B Q3K or even 119B Q3K fit with headroom).

5. COMPARISON with what is MEASURED on GORAST (card 05 only, coder Jab Q4_K_M 160 experts, 26.4 GB, REF 15600, 900 mV, greedy 32K):
- MTP5 + f16 KV + dio: PP 198 / TG 17.7 (steady ~19.7); projected 90K PP ~134.
- + expert cache 16 slots / 4 inserts / draft experts on CPU: TG 23.8 (steady ~25.5), PP 168 (90K ~107). 8K cache screen running now.
- ngram speculation: dead (fake gain on a quoting prompt, worse on real code edits).
Niwaki 102B reads ~5.3B active params/token at ~3.5 bpw vs the coder's ~3B active at ~4.8 bpw -> similar or slightly MORE bytes per token, so I would NOT expect it to be faster than the coder on the old board even with enough RAM; its argument is quality (near-full-model, all 512 experts kept in half the layers) rather than speed.
